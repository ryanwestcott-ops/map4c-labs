One-variable data

One fact is recorded about each person or thing. Ask yourself: how many facts do I know about each one? One fact means one-variable data.

Example: 20 students each report their number of siblings. The variable is number of siblings. Two-variable data (like height and shoe size) comes later.

Types of data

Categorical

Labels or groups.

favourite sport, phone brand, home form

Discrete

Counted. Only certain values, with gaps.

siblings, goals scored, pets

Continuous

Measured. Any value in a range.

height, time, mass, temperature

Quick test: could the value be 2.5 or 2.57? If yes, it's continuous. If you count it, it's discrete.

Choosing the right graph

Tally and frequency

A tally counts as you go. Every fifth mark crosses the four before it. The frequency is the count.

Home formTallyFrequency

For continuous data, group values into equal intervals such as "10 to under 20". A value of exactly 20 goes in "20 to under 30".

Descriptive statements

Use real numbers from the data.

  • The most common ___ was ___ (___ people).
  • ___ out of ___ students ...
  • Values ranged from ___ to ___.
  • ___ had about twice as many as ___.
  • One value, ___, is far from the rest.
Example: "8 of the 20 students have exactly 1 sibling, which is the most common answer."
